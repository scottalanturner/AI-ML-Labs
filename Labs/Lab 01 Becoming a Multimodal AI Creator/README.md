# Becoming a Multimodal AI Creator

**60-Minute Individual Hands-On Exercise**

## Overview

This exercise introduces you to multimodal AI capabilities through hands-on creation using free tools. You'll work individually from home to create AI-generated content across text, image, and audio formats.

## Required Tools (All Free)

- Claude.ai (https://claude.ai) or ChatGPT (https://chat.openai.com)
- Microsoft Copilot (https://copilot.microsoft.com) or Playground AI (https://playground.com)
- Suno.ai (https://suno.ai) - 3 free songs daily
- Ideogram.ai (https://ideogram.ai) or Leonardo.ai (https://leonardo.ai)
- Canva (https://canva.com) - free account
- Your smartphone/computer camera

## Part 1: The AI Detective Challenge (30 minutes)

### Learning Objectives

- Understand how different AI models "see" and interpret the world
- Learn prompt refinement through iteration
- Experience the gap between human and AI perception

### Activity: "Lost in Translation"

#### Step 1: Choose Your Subject (3 minutes)

- Find an interesting corner of your living space (bookshelf, desk, kitchen counter, window view)
- Take a photo with your phone
- Don't pick something too simple - complexity makes this more interesting!

#### Step 2: The Telephone Game (12 minutes)

1. Upload your photo to Claude/ChatGPT
2. Ask: "Describe this image in detail as if explaining it to someone who can't see it"
3. Copy that description to a new chat (without the image)
4. Ask: "Based on this description, write a more artistic, evocative description suitable for image generation"
5. Take that artistic description to Copilot/Playground AI
6. Generate 3 variations of the image
7. Document what changed between reality and AI interpretation

#### Step 3: Reverse Engineering (10 minutes)

1. Take your favorite AI-generated variation
2. Upload it to Claude/ChatGPT (new conversation)
3. Ask: "What story does this image tell? What happened just before and after this moment?"
4. Generate one more image based on the "what happened next" description
5. Create a simple before/after story pair

#### Step 4: Reflection Journal (5 minutes)

Quick-write responses to:

- What details did the AI emphasize that you didn't notice?
- What got completely lost or transformed?
- How did the "mood" change through the translations?

**Real-World Connection:** This mirrors how Netflix uses AI to generate different thumbnail images for the same show based on viewer preferences - the AI emphasizes different visual elements to tell different stories.

## Part 2: The "Spotify Wrapped" Personal Poster Project (30 minutes)

### Learning Objectives

- Create multimodal content combining audio inspiration, visuals, and text
- Understand AI customization and style transfer
- Experience rapid prototyping with AI tools

### Activity: "Your Week as an Album"

#### Step 1: Create Your Title Track (7 minutes)

1. In Claude/ChatGPT, write: "I want to create an album about my week. Here's what happened: [describe 2-3 memorable moments from your week]"
2. Ask it to: "Turn this into a creative album title and 4 song titles that tell this story"
3. Pick your favorite song title
4. Go to Suno.ai and generate a 30-second instrumental track (no lyrics needed)
   - Use descriptive prompts like "upbeat morning coffee shop jazz" or "late night study lo-fi"
5. Download the audio (you'll submit the link, not use it in the poster)

#### Step 2: Design Your Album Cover (10 minutes)

1. Take your album title to Ideogram/Leonardo
2. Generate an album cover with this formula:
   - "[Your album title] album cover art, [pick a style: minimalist/vintage/neon/watercolor/collage], [pick a mood: dreamy/energetic/mysterious/nostalgic]"
3. Generate 3-4 variations
4. Pick your favorite

#### Step 3: Create the Back Cover - Song List Visual (8 minutes)

1. Return to Claude/ChatGPT with your 4 song titles
2. Ask: "For each of these 4 songs, give me:
   - A one-line lyric preview
   - Three emojis that capture the vibe
   - A color that represents the mood"
3. Go to Canva (free template: "Album Cover" or "Poster")
4. Create a simple design with:
   - Your AI-generated album cover as the main image
   - Your album title
   - The 4 song titles with their emoji combinations
   - A "Produced by [Your Name] × AI" credit

#### Step 4: The Artist's Statement (5 minutes)

Below your Canva poster (or in a separate doc), write a brief "artist's statement":

- One sentence about what your week's "album" represents
- Which AI surprised you most with its interpretation
- Rate each AI tool's contribution (1-5 stars)
- Your Suno track link (just paste the URL)

**Real-World Connection:** This process is exactly how indie musicians are using AI to rapidly prototype album concepts. Artists like Taryn Southern have released entire AI-collaborative albums, and major labels use similar workflows for concept development.

## Deliverables

Submit ONE document containing:

### 1. Part 1 Output: A collage showing:

- Your original photo
- The AI description (text)
- 3 AI regenerated images
- Your final story image

### 2. Part 2 Output:

- Your finished Canva "album poster" exported as an image
- The Suno.ai link to your generated track

### 3. Reflection (200 words): Answer these questions:

- Which AI tool felt most like a collaborator vs. a tool?
- What would you do differently with unlimited time?
- Could you tell this was AI-generated if you didn't know?

## Troubleshooting Guide

### Common Issues & Solutions

**If Suno is at capacity:**

- Try Boomy.ai (instant access) or Soundraw.ai
- Or skip audio and create a "silent album" concept

**If image generation is slow:**

- Try different times (early morning is fastest)
- Use Bing Image Creator as backup
- Reduce quality settings to "fast" mode

**If Canva feels overwhelming:**

- Use Google Drawings or even Google Docs
- The design doesn't need to be perfect - focus on the concept

### Pro Tips

- Save all prompts in a document - you'll want to iterate
- Download generations as you go (don't rely on browser history)
- Late night/early morning = less traffic on free tools
- Keep browser tabs to a minimum to prevent crashes

## Academic Integrity Note

This exercise is about exploring AI as a creative tool. All AI use should be clearly attributed. Your reflection and analysis must be your own original thoughts. Remember: we're learning to work WITH AI, not having AI work FOR us.
